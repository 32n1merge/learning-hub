Safe driving: make room for mistakes

Defensive driving is not timid driving. It is continuously creating time, space, and options for hazards you can see—and ones you cannot.

The space-cushion routine

  1. Look far ahead, then scan mirrors and the sides of the road.
  2. Keep a following interval appropriate to conditions; increase it for rain, snow, darkness, traffic, and large vehicles.
  3. Before braking, turning, or changing lanes: mirror, signal, shoulder-check, then move only when safe.
  4. Adjust speed to conditions even when you are below the posted maximum.
Colorado weather reality: ice can appear on bridges, overpasses, shaded spots, and at intersections. On slippery surfaces, reduce speed early, increase following distance, avoid abrupt inputs, and brake smoothly. Four-wheel drive helps traction; it does not shorten physics.

Never treat impairment as negotiable

Alcohol, cannabis, medications, fatigue, and distracted driving reduce perception and reaction time. The correct test answer is nearly always: do not drive impaired or distracted; arrange another ride or wait. Colorado has strict consequences for under-21 alcohol violations and prohibits handheld-phone use while driving except as specifically allowed by law.

Sharing the road

  • Give motorcycles a full lane; check mirrors and blind spots carefully.
  • Pass bicyclists only when safe, leaving legally required clearance.
  • Never drive around a lowered railroad gate; stop and look/listen when required.
  • Move over or slow down as law and conditions require for stopped emergency, tow, and maintenance vehicles.
