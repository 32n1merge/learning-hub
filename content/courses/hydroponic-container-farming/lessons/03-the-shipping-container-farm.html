📦 The Shipping Container Farm

A shipping container isn't just a metal box — it's a blank canvas for a controlled-environment farm. But turning a steel shipping container into a thriving grow space requires careful planning. Get the insulation, layout, and airflow right, and you've got a precision agriculture machine. Get them wrong, and you've got an expensive oven.

The Four Layers of Container Prep

1. Insulation — Your #1 Priority

Steel conducts heat beautifully — which is terrible for a farm. Without insulation, your container becomes a solar oven in summer and a freezer in winter. The gold standard: closed-cell spray foam (R-7 per inch) applied 2–3 inches thick to walls and ceiling. This gives you R-14 to R-21, seals air gaps, and acts as a vapor barrier.

⚠️ Don't skip the floor: Concrete slabs wick cold from the ground. Insulate the floor with rigid foam board (XPS, R-10 minimum) under plywood decking. Cold roots = slow growth, even if the air is warm.

2. Interior Wall Finish

Over the spray foam, install FRP (fiberglass-reinforced plastic) panels or food-grade white poly panels. They're reflective (bouncing light back to plants), waterproof, mold-resistant, and cleanable. No drywall — it's a humidity nightmare waiting to happen.

3. Cut Openings Strategically

  • HVAC penetration: One 12" hole near the closed end for a mini-split air handler
  • Intake/exhaust fans: 6–8" ducted holes with backdraft dampers
  • Electrical entry: Weatherproof conduit entry for power + data cables
  • Between-containers duct: (See Lesson 8!) A 8–10" duct connecting the two containers for air exchange

4. Flooring & Drainage

The floor should slope slightly (1/4" per foot) toward a central trench drain, or use individual catchment trays under each system. All runoff gets filtered and returned to the main reservoir — nothing wasted.
